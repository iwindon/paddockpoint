Thunderstorm Electronics Safety Tips and What to Do After a Lightning Strike

Strong storms can cause power surges and outages that may damage TVs, Wi-Fi routers, gaming systems, computers, and other home electronics. A few simple steps before and after a storm can reduce risk and speed up recovery.

Before and during a thunderstorm:

  • Use quality surge protectors for TVs, routers, modems, computers, and entertainment equipment.
  • Unplug high-value electronics when severe lightning is nearby and it is safe to do so.
  • Unplug coax and Ethernet lines from modems and routers if possible, since surges can travel through data lines.
  • Avoid using corded electronics and do not handle plugged-in equipment during active lightning.
  • Keep devices elevated and dry in case heavy rain causes leaks or moisture issues.

If devices are not working after a lightning strike:

  1. Check for a neighborhood outage first and confirm whether your utility provider has reported a service interruption.
  2. Reset tripped breakers by switching each tripped breaker fully to OFF, then back to ON.
  3. Check and reset any GFCI outlets in bathrooms, garages, kitchens, and exterior outlets, as they can cut power to nearby rooms.
  4. Power cycle your internet equipment: unplug modem and router for 60 seconds, plug modem in first, wait for it to reconnect, then plug in the router.
  5. Test devices in a known working outlet and avoid repeatedly powering on equipment that smells burnt or shows signs of damage.
  6. If internet service is still down, contact your ISP to check for line damage or to test your modem remotely.

If you suspect panel damage, burning odor, sparking, or warm outlets, stop troubleshooting and contact a licensed electrician immediately. Safety should always come first.

Community Safety Reminder: Stop Signs, Speed Limits, and Safe Parking Practices

Good morning Paddock Point.

We would like to remind everyone of the importance of following traffic laws and practicing safe driving habits within our community. This morning, there was a serious near-miss incident in which an individual was almost struck by a vehicle when a driver failed to stop at a posted stop sign.

Stop signs and speed limits are in place to protect pedestrians, children, pets, and motorists. Taking a few extra seconds to come to a complete stop and driving at a safe, legal speed can prevent a tragic accident and help keep our neighborhood safe.

We would also like to ask residents to be mindful when parking vehicles on community streets. For those who live on corners, near community entrances, and adjacent to stop signs, please exercise courtesy and consider how parked vehicles may affect visibility and traffic flow. Many of us have either witnessed or experienced situations where parked vehicles have obstructed sight lines, making it difficult to see oncoming traffic or requiring drivers to maneuver around vehicles in the roadway, creating potentially dangerous conditions.

To help improve safety for everyone, please:

  • Come to a complete stop at all stop signs.
  • Obey posted speed limits throughout the community.
  • Be alert for pedestrians, cyclists, children, and other vehicles.
  • Avoid distracted driving.
  • Park in a manner that does not obstruct visibility at intersections, entrances, or stop signs.
  • Be considerate of your neighbors and the shared roadways within our community.

Our goal is to ensure the safety and well-being of all residents, families, guests, and visitors. By working together and demonstrating courtesy behind the wheel and when parking, we can help prevent accidents and maintain a safe environment for everyone.

Thank you for your cooperation and commitment to keeping our community safe. Please slow down, stop completely at all stop signs, and park responsibly so that we can all do our part to protect one another. Stay safe and look out for your neighbors.

How to Report a Non-Emergency Safety Concern

If you observe concerning activity in the neighborhood, please use the following steps to make a clear report.

  1. For immediate danger or urgent situations, call 911.
  2. For non-emergency concerns, call the Spartanburg County non-emergency line at 864-596-2222.
  3. Provide the location and a brief factual description of what you observed.
  4. The non-emergency number connects you to an operator, who will route the call and dispatch the appropriate response.
  5. Depending on the situation, follow-up may be by phone, or an officer/deputy may be sent to your home if needed.
  6. If you have additional neighborhood concerns, you may also notify the HOA Board for documentation.
